# User Needs & Jobs to be Done (JTBD)

This document maps out the specific user needs, Jobs to be Done (JTBD), and critical friction points for professional users preparing to quit smoking (represented by Ek).

---

## 🎯 Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

We use the JTBD framework to focus on the core outcomes the user wants to achieve:

### 1. The Core Functional Job
> **"When I** decide to prepare for smoking cessation, **I want to** build a highly structured, step-by-step quit plan, **so that** I can manage withdrawal symptoms and cravings systematically without impacting my day-to-day business operations."

### 2. The Emotional/Social Job (Discretion & Reputational Integrity)
> **"When I** seek information and professional support to quit, **I want to** engage with tools and consultants anonymously and confidentially, **so that** my employees, clients, and industry peers remain completely unaware of my habit and recovery process."

### 3. The Contextual Micro-Job (Acute Craving Mitigation)
> **"When I** experience a sudden, intense nicotine urge during a high-stress workday, **I want to** access rapid, discreet physical or mental exercises, **so that** I can regain my focus immediately without calling attention to myself in the office."

---

## 💡 User Needs Summary

*   **Self-Paced Information:** Needs to digest evidence-based methods, timeline projections, and clinical stats at his own convenience.
*   **Absolute Privacy Assurance:** Needs explicit, prominent statements explaining that data is stored locally and chats are end-to-end encrypted.
*   **Asynchronous & Masked Communication:** Needs channels that fit into a busy calendar and protect his identity (e.g., chat boxes or calendar bookings with masked intro protocols).
*   **Direct & Action-Oriented Interface:** Needs a clean, high-density dashboard that uses bullet points, checklists, and calculators rather than long narratives.

---

## ⚠️ Friction Points & Usability Pitfalls

| Friction Point | User Impact | UI/UX Remedy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mandatory Sign-Up Gates** | The user will immediately abandon the site if forced to input an email or create an account to view content. | Provide 100% ungated access to all quit planning calculators and coping toolkits. |
| **Intrusive Cold-Calling** | Traditional support setups call the user at random hours, risking exposure during meetings or in front of staff. | Implement a strict scheduled call-back booking system where the user chooses the exact hour. |
| **Conspicuous Branding** | High-contrast banners or graphic medical images of damaged lungs make browsing in public or open offices highly risky. | Use corporate-style, clean typography, neutral color palettes, and professional headings. |
| **Long-Form Onboarding** | Extensive forms with detailed history questions feel like a waste of time to a busy business owner. | Use progressive disclosure and interactive sliders to gather input quickly and incrementally. |
